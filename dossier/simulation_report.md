# Simulation of the analysis procedure: certifying run

This report documents the certifying run of `full_simulation.py`, completed on October 31, 2026. It supersedes the design-check run of October 28, 2026, which used reduced replication counts. No experimental data exist, and every number below comes from data generated by the declared measurement model.

## What is simulated

The generator is the measurement model declared in Section 10.1 of the protocol. Individual intercepts and individual contractions are bivariate normal. The logit of the subjective posterior combines the prior and the certificates seen so far with estimated weights. Stated thresholds load on the center of the price grid through an anchoring coefficient, which is identified by the random assignment of the 20-90 and 30-100 formats. The error of each list has a factor common to the two lists of the same trial, with true correlation 0.30, and responses are censored to unit intervals and to the grid edges. In protocol S the intermediate measurement falls at the prior or after the first certificate, balanced over priors and selection intensities.

For the first hypothesis the estimator is the interval-censored likelihood, integrated by Gauss-Hermite quadrature with seven nodes for each individual effect and five for the common error factor, and the test is the one-sided likelihood-ratio test. The studentized dyad bootstrap of Section 10.1, which remains the declared primary procedure, is computed on one example dataset, because repeating it inside every replication would require about four million likelihood maximizations. For the second hypothesis the generator adds dyad and session effects, builds the observable policy index of Section 10.2, averages it by dyad, and applies the session fixed-effects regression with the two one-sided tests. The pilot rule is simulated on 36 subjects with the analytic power formula of Section 12.2.

## Run configuration

Each scenario of both hypotheses uses 2,000 replications, the pilot rule uses 2,000 simulated pilots, and the example bootstrap uses 1,999 replications. Seeds are fixed and independent of the number of worker processes. The run used eight processes on an Apple silicon laptop, about two hours per scenario of the first hypothesis, minutes for the second hypothesis and the pilot rule, and about ninety minutes for the bootstrap. A second complete execution of the first hypothesis with the same seeds reproduced every number exactly.

## First hypothesis

| Scenario, 100 subjects in protocol S | Asymptotic threshold 3.841 | Calibrated threshold 4.279 | Mean estimate | RMSE |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| No contraction, no anchoring | 3.20% | 2.50% | -0.003 | 0.031 |
| No contraction, anchoring 0.10 | 3.20% | 2.25% | -0.003 | 0.034 |
| Contraction 0.15, anchoring 0.10 | 99.60% | 99.45% | 0.147 | 0.030 |
| Contraction 0.25, anchoring 0.10 | 100% | 100% | 0.248 | 0.030 |
| Contraction 0.15, list error 6 | 90.05% | 87.95% | 0.145 | 0.047 |

The asymptotic test rejects a true null in 3.2 percent of replications, with Monte Carlo upper bound 0.040, which exceeds the tolerance of 0.035 set in Section 12.3. The chi-square approximation is mildly liberal with ten nuisance parameters and 100 subjects. The critical value is therefore calibrated by simulation, as the quantile of the likelihood-ratio statistic among null replications without anchoring and with a positive estimate that yields a one-sided level of 2.5 percent, and it equals 4.279. Applied to the 2,000 independent replications with anchoring, which played no role in the calibration, the calibrated test rejects in 2.25 percent of cases with upper bound 0.029. Power at a contraction of 0.15 falls only from 99.60 to 99.45 percent. With real data the same calibration becomes a parametric bootstrap of the likelihood-ratio statistic under the null at the estimated nuisance parameters, and the calibrated test accompanies the dyad bootstrap as a secondary check.

Estimates of the contraction are centered, with absolute bias at most 0.006, and the anchoring coefficient is recovered at 0.10 in every scenario. The error correlation between the two lists is estimated between 0.07 and 0.13 against a true value of 0.30. It is weakly identified by eight trials per subject and it does not move the contraction estimate.

On the example dataset with true contraction 0.15, the point estimate is 0.129, the likelihood-ratio statistic is 15.7, and the 2.5 percent lower bounds of the dyad bootstrap are 0.061 in the basic version and 0.052 in the percentile version, both above zero.

## Second hypothesis and the pilot rule

| Scenario, 300 subjects | True difference zero | True difference one point | Implied individual SD | Dyad ICC |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| List error 4, dyad effect 2 | 99.7% | 90.1% | 3.9 | 0.21 |
| List error 6, dyad effect 3 | 95.9% | 76.4% | 4.6 | 0.32 |

The criteria of Section 12.3 for the equivalence test, power of at least 0.8 at a true difference of zero and 0.6 at a true difference of one point, both on the Monte Carlo lower bound, are met at 300 subjects in both scenarios. The pilot rule selects 300 in all 2,000 simulated pilots, with mean realized power 0.997, because the individual standard deviation implied by the structural model stays below the threshold of 5.92 in both scenarios. The sample-size constraint binds only if the list error exceeds roughly eight points, which is the quantity the pilot measures in the certain-value tasks.

## Outcome

Every criterion of Section 12.3 is met with the calibrated critical value, and the two criteria on type-one error fail only at the asymptotic one. The command `python3 full_simulation.py criteria` prints the full list and writes it to `design_data/criteria_12_3.csv`. The per-replication values of every scenario of the first hypothesis are in `design_data/replications_H1_*.csv`, and the calibration is in `design_data/calibrated_H1.csv`.

## Limits

The size of the dyad bootstrap itself is not certified by this run, for the computational reason given above, and the example dataset shows only that the two procedures agree in one case. The quadrature grid is fixed rather than adaptive. Both points are natural targets for a second version of the program, with an analytic gradient and adaptive quadrature in the sense of Pinheiro and Bates (1995), which would reduce the run time by about an order of magnitude.
